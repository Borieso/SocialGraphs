It's a Wikipedia speedrun

In the real version, sometimes called a wikirace, you click from one Wikipedia article to another. You can only use the links on the page, and you try to do it as fast as you can. This game works the same way, but it only uses 229 Marvel characters from week 1. These are the characters that can all reach each other by following links.

  • At each page, you see up to 3 possible links. You never see the whole article, so you cannot just scan it for the best link.
  • At least one of the 3 links always brings you closer to the target. Sometimes more than one does. Any link that does not is a decoy.
  • Click a link to follow it. Keep going until you reach the target.
  • At the end, your number of clicks is compared to par. Par is the shortest path. The game finds it with a method called breadth-first search as soon as the round starts.

Stuck? Use Try again to restart the same round. Use New characters to get a new pair.

Is this fair?

Mostly, but there is one thing worth knowing. The Connected to text under each card lists only 3 of that character's real links on Wikipedia. Some characters have over 100 links, so we only show the 3 that are the rarest, meaning the least likely to be a big hub like Spider-Man.

  • Because of this, the text will not always mention the link that got you to a card. It also will not always mention the link that would be the best next move.
  • Read it as background information about the character, not as a hint about which card to pick.
  • The cards themselves are picked automatically. If 3 or fewer links would move you closer to the target, all of them are shown. Some characters have more than 3 such links at once (Spider-Man often does); when that happens, only 3 can fit on the board, so one you might expect can still be left out.
  • Sometimes you will only see 1 or 2 cards instead of 3. That happens when a character's real Wikipedia page has few outgoing links. It is a property of the real network, not a bug.
