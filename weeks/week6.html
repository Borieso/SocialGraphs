Under J&M a shared word has almost no chance. The book takes the log of the count, so piling up occurrences barely helps, while every extra week that uses the word cuts its idf hard. A word in three weeks has to appear 659 times to tie a df = 1 word seen three times. Under the course formula it needs about 2.6 times as many, or 8. That is why the J&M list is green from top to bottom in every week.

This is probably mostly down to our small corpus. With only six documents, idf can take just six values, and log10(6/df) drops to almost nothing after a few weeks. With hundreds of documents, sharing a word with a handful of others would hardly matter. Even so, both lists do a good job of pulling out what each week is about: pagerank, closeness and eigenvector for week 3, philosophers, partition and dendrogram for week 4, tokenization and subword for week 5, topics, embedding and lda for week 6.

How alike are the weeks?

The matrix splits the course in two. The first four weeks, all about networks, are more alike among themselves (0.07 to 0.16) than with weeks 5 and 6 (0.02 to 0.05). Weeks 5 and 6 are by far the most alike pair at 0.33, which is what we would expect, since week 6 builds further on the NLP from week 5 and keeps using its words: corpus, word, language. J&M shows the same pattern, only weaker, because it gives less TF-IDF weight to words that occur in several weeks. Those shared words are exactly what pushes the similarities higher under the course formula.

Make the scale balance

Both words sit in the same week, so they share the same |d|. The only things that differ are the count and the idf.

  • The green word appears in this week only, so df = 1 and its idf is ln(6/1) ≈ 1.79, the highest any word can get. You see its full TF-IDF.
  • The orange word also appears in other weeks (the filled dots). The more weeks share it, the smaller its idf gets. A word in all six weeks gets ln(6/6) = 0, and no count can save it.
  • Guess the count that would give the orange word the same TF-IDF as the green one. Use the slider or type the number.
  • Since |d| cancels out, the exact answer is
    countorange = countgreen × ln(6/1) ÷ ln(6/df)
    Counts are whole numbers, so the nearest whole number gets the full 100 points.
  • With Course + J&M you type two numbers, one per formula. J&M takes the log of the count, so its answers can run into powers of ten: type 1e12 or 10^12 for 1012.

After each round you also see how often the orange word really appears in that week, and whether that is enough to beat the green one.
